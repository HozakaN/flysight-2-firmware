FlySight 2 firmware: three BLE fixes proposed for merge

Prepared by Guillaume Imbert · 8 October 2026 · checked against origin/master (9ec7186) and origin/develop (e67ee9f). The same patch applies to both.

The problem that started this

  1. Pair to an unpaired Mac.
  2. Disconnect.
  3. Start pairing on the FlySight.
ExpectedThe FlySight is found by the Mac and the Mac is able to connect to the FlySight.
ActualThe Mac cannot connect to the FlySight.
AndroidThe issue does not appear on Android.
Hypothesis: the difference between the platformsmacOS hides the real MAC address and cannot remap it after the FlySight pairing process restarts.

Why start a new pairing process. To trigger advertising when the FlySight does not restart it. A bug was found about that (bug 2). During development it also makes the work a lot faster.

What the investigation found

  • The bug is confirmed. After these three steps the Mac cannot connect to the FlySight, exactly as reported. It was reproduced on unmodified master and develop, and with the fix the Mac connects (bug 1).
  • Its cause is not the suspected one. The Mac does recognise the FlySight: once bonded, it shows it under one and the same identifier when the FlySight is idle, when it is back in pairing mode, and after it reboots. What fails is the connection request itself, which the FlySight's radio controller discards before the firmware hears of it.
  • Android and iOS are not affected. An Android phone and an iPad were tested: both go through the same sequence on unmodified firmware. They connect in a different way from the Mac, which the controller accepts. The difference between the platforms is in the address the host connects from, not in how it identifies the FlySight.
  • A FlySight that stops advertising was reproduced on develop: a pairing window that expires unused ends with no advertising at all (bug 2). This one concerns every platform. On develop the two chain for a Mac: the bonded Mac cannot connect during the window, the window expires unused, and the FlySight goes silent.
  • A third, unrelated defect was found on the way (bug 3).

An app-side issue, not a firmware one. For a host that is not bonded, the identifier does change. The FlySight advertises from a private address that changes each time advertising restarts, and only a bonded host can follow it. Before it was paired, the iPad listed the same FlySight under four different identifiers over four advertising runs. An entry obtained before pairing mode started is therefore dead once pairing mode starts: an app that still offers it, or that has saved it, waits forever on an identifier that no longer exists. This is how BLE privacy is meant to behave, so the handling belongs in the app: its scan list must only hold what is advertising now, and it should not keep a FlySight it has never connected to.

1.Bug 1: a bonded Mac can no longer connect

Glossary

Identity address
The permanent address of a Bluetooth device. A Mac's is its public address.
Resolvable private address (RPA)
A temporary address that a device uses instead of its identity address, and changes regularly. It is derived from a secret key, the IRK; only a peer that holds the IRK can tell which device is behind it.
Bonding
Pairing, then storing the keys. Each side gives the other its identity address and its IRK.
Resolving list
A table in the radio controller: one entry per bonded peer, with its identity address and IRK, so the controller can recognise that peer's RPAs by itself. The firmware fills it in ble_count_bonded_devices().
Privacy mode of a resolving-list entry
Defined by the Bluetooth specification (HCI command LE Set Privacy Mode). Network Privacy is the default: the controller only answers that peer when it uses an RPA, and ignores a connection request made from its identity address. Device Privacy: the controller accepts both.
Filter accept list
The list of bonded devices allowed to connect outside pairing mode. A separate mechanism from the resolving list.

Problem

What the user sees. The first pairing with a Mac works. After that, the Mac cannot connect to that FlySight again, whether the FlySight is idle or back in pairing mode. On the Mac the attempt simply never completes. The FlySight shows nothing either.

To reproduce (master or develop, with a macOS app using CoreBluetooth):

  1. Double-press to enter pairing mode, connect from the Mac, accept the pairing request.
  2. Disconnect.
  3. Try to connect again from the Mac, with the FlySight idle, or after a new double-press. The connection never happens.

What the firmware sees. An instrumented build recorded every HCI event delivered to SVCCTL_App_Notification(). During the Mac's attempts it recorded nothing at all: no connection event, no failure. With the fix below, the connection event appears, and shows how the two kinds of central differ:

# Mac
LE Enhanced Connection Complete   peer = FC:B2:14:**:**:** (public address)          peer RPA = none
# Android phone (accepted with or without the fix)
LE Enhanced Connection Complete   peer = 64:9D:38:**:**:** (identity, resolved)      peer RPA = 75:D8:EE:25:A8:07
# iPad (accepted with or without the fix)
LE Enhanced Connection Complete   peer = 4C:56:9D:**:**:** (identity, resolved)      peer RPA = 45:C5:A6:6F:63:53

The Android phone and the iPad connect from an RPA, which the controller resolves to their identity. The Mac connects from its identity address, with no RPA.

Cause. Each time advertising starts, ble_count_bonded_devices() rebuilds the controller's tables:

STM32_WPAN/App/app_ble.c
ret = aci_gap_configure_whitelist();                               // accept list  = bonded devices
ret = aci_gap_get_bonded_devices(&total, devices);
...
ret = aci_gap_add_devices_to_resolving_list(total, rl_entries, 1); // resolving list = bonded devices

Entries are added in the default Network Privacy mode. From the moment the Mac is bonded, the controller therefore drops its connection requests, because they carry its identity address. Nothing is reported to the application, which is why the bug is invisible from the firmware side.

  • The first pairing works because the resolving list is still empty.
  • Pairing mode does not help: it turns the accept list off, but the privacy rule belongs to the resolving list and still applies.
  • Android and the iPad tested are not affected, because they connect from an RPA.

Proposed fix

Put each bonded peer's entry in Device Privacy mode, right after the resolving list is filled. The list is rebuilt at every advertising start, so the setting is re-applied each time.

STM32_WPAN/App/app_ble.c — ble_count_bonded_devices()
   ret = aci_gap_add_devices_to_resolving_list(total, rl_entries, 1);
   if (ret != BLE_STATUS_SUCCESS)
   {
     ...
     return -1;
   }
+
+  // 4) Let bonded peers connect with their identity address too. Entries of the
+  //    resolving list default to Network Privacy mode, where the controller
+  //    silently ignores a connection request that does not use a resolvable
+  //    private address. A Mac connects from its public identity address
+  //    (measured on macOS 26), so without this a bonded Mac never reaches us,
+  //    in pairing mode or not.
+  for (uint8_t k = 0; k < total; k++)
+  {
+    ret = hci_le_set_privacy_mode(rl_entries[k].Peer_Identity_Address_Type,
+                                  rl_entries[k].Peer_Identity_Address,
+                                  BLE_PRIVACY_MODE_DEVICE);
+    if (ret != BLE_STATUS_SUCCESS)
+    {
+      APP_DBG_MSG("hci_le_set_privacy_mode fail %x\r\n", ret);
+    }
+  }

BLE_PRIVACY_MODE_DEVICE is a new local constant, 0x01, the value defined by the specification.

What changes, and what does not. The FlySight keeps advertising with RPAs, and the accept list keeps working as before. The one thing that widens: a connection request carrying a bonded peer's identity address is now accepted at link level. Whoever sends it still cannot read or write anything, because the FlySight's characteristics require an encrypted link and encryption needs the bond keys; a link that stays idle is closed by the existing 30-second inactivity timeout.

2.Bug 2: an unused pairing window ends in silence

Glossary

Pairing window
The 30 seconds after a double-press, during which request_pairing is set: the FlySight advertises the pairing flag (01) and the accept list is off.
Fast and low-power advertising
FS_Adv_Request(APP_BLE_FAST_ADV) advertises every 80–100 ms and starts a 30-second timer. When the timer fires, Adv_Update() calls FS_Adv_Request(APP_BLE_LP_ADV), which restarts advertising at the slow rate with no timer.
Limited discoverable mode
The stack mode used for pairing advertising (aci_gap_set_limited_discoverable()). The stack ends it by itself after about three minutes and raises ACI_GAP_LIMITED_DISCOVERABLE_VSEVT_CODE. Normal advertising (aci_gap_set_undirected_connectable()) has no such limit.
Sequencer tasks "with" and "without" HCI command
The two task lists in app_conf.h. While one HCI command waits for its answer, BLE_StatusNot() pauses every task of the first list; tasks of the second list keep running.

Problem

What the user sees. Double-press, then nobody connects. After 30 seconds the LED goes off as expected. But a scanner still sees the FlySight advertising "pairing mode" for another three minutes, and after that the FlySight cannot be found or reached at all, by any device, until the button is pressed again. Unplugging and replugging USB does not bring it back.

Measured (double-press at t = 0, nobody connects, a scanner listens for 250 s):

develop, unmodifieddevelop, with the fix
0 to 30 spairing flag 01pairing flag 01
30 s to about 200 sstill flag 01, accept list offnormal advertising, flag 00
after about 200 sno advertising at allnormal advertising continues

Cause. When the 30-second timer fires during a pairing window:

  1. Adv_Update() calls FS_Adv_Request(APP_BLE_LP_ADV). That function calls the pairing callback, which only queues an event for the mode task, then goes on. request_pairing is still 1, so advertising restarts in limited discoverable mode: pairing configuration, at the slow rate, with no timer.
  2. The mode task then runs, leaves pairing mode (LED off) and calls APP_BLE_CancelPairing(). That function clears request_pairing, but only restarts advertising if (... == APP_BLE_FAST_ADV). The state is now APP_BLE_LP_ADV, so advertising is left as it is.
  3. About three minutes later the stack ends limited discoverable mode. The application does not handle that event, and no timer is running, so nothing ever starts advertising again.

Why master does not show it. On master, CFG_TASK_FS_MODE_UPDATE_ID is in the list of tasks without HCI command. The mode task is therefore not paused: it runs while FS_Adv_Request() is still waiting for the answer to its first command, and clears request_pairing just before FS_Adv_Request() tests it. Step 1 then starts normal advertising, by accident of timing. On develop, commit 7b57cc8 ("Classify mode update task as using HCI", 13 July 2026) moved the task to the other list. That change is right, since the mode task does send HCI commands, but it removed the accident, and the sequence above now happens as written. Measured: on unmodified master the flag returns to 00 at 30 s and advertising continues.

When it appeared. With the work that lets a central ask for a mode change over BLE. That work is commit cff0b12 ("Add device reset and firmware update from BLE", 9 May 2026): it adds the Device State commands that reboot the device, install an uploaded firmware or request a mode, and the mode task carries them out. Two months later 7b57cc8 moved the mode task to the tasks with HCI command, presumably for that reason. Measured on develop, same sequence, scanner listening for 75 s:

CommitBLE mode requestsMode taskResult
4fd8d08, the commit just before 7b57cc8presentwithout HCI commandflag 01 for 30 s, then 00
7b57cc8presentwith HCI commandflag 01 for the whole 75 s

So the symptom arrived with 7b57cc8. The code at fault in app_ble.c is older than both commits; the reclassification is what exposed it.

Proposed fix

The pairing window ends when the fast-advertising timer fires. Clear the request at that point, before advertising is restarted:

STM32_WPAN/App/app_ble.c
 static void Adv_Update(void)
 {
+  /*
+   * The fast advertising period is over, and with it any pairing window. The
+   * mode task has not left pairing mode yet at this point, so clear the request
+   * here: otherwise advertising restarts in pairing configuration (limited
+   * discoverable, pairing flag set, accept list off) and nothing restarts it in
+   * normal configuration afterwards.
+   */
+  request_pairing = 0;
+
   FS_Adv_Request(APP_BLE_LP_ADV);
 }

Advertising then restarts once, directly in normal configuration. The mode task still leaves pairing mode through the same callback as before.

Why here. This does not depend on which task list the mode task is in, so it is correct on both branches. An earlier attempt restarted advertising from APP_BLE_CancelPairing() when the state is APP_BLE_LP_ADV; it worked on develop, but on master that function can run while another HCI command is still pending, so it was dropped.

Possible addition, not included: handling ACI_GAP_LIMITED_DISCOVERABLE_VSEVT_CODE by restarting normal advertising would guarantee the FlySight can never stay silent, whatever path leads there.

3.Bug 3: all-zero BLE keys on a first boot without flysight.txt

Glossary

BLE_IRK and BLE_ERK
The two root keys stored in flysight.txt and given to the stack at start-up (CONFIG_DATA_IR_OFFSET, CONFIG_DATA_ER_OFFSET). The first one determines how bonded devices recognise the FlySight; the second one is used for the link keys. FS_State_Read() generates them randomly when they are all zero.

Problem

FS_State_Read() returns as soon as flysight.txt cannot be opened, before the code that generates the keys:

FlySight/state.c
memset(state.ble_irk, 0, CONFIG_DATA_IR_LEN);
memset(state.ble_erk, 0, CONFIG_DATA_ER_LEN);
...
if (f_open(&stateFile, "/flysight.txt", FA_READ) != FR_OK)
    return;                                  // keys stay all zero for this boot
...
/* Initialize IRK if needed */             // never reached in that case
while (is_all_zeros(state.ble_irk, CONFIG_DATA_IR_LEN)) { ... }

So on a first boot with no file (a new card, or a file deleted to reset the device, which Docs/ble.md suggests), the stack runs with all-zero root keys and the new flysight.txt is written with zeros. At the following boot the file exists, the zeros are replaced by random keys, and the FlySight's identity changes. A device bonded during that first boot can be expected not to recognise the FlySight afterwards. The device ID is skipped the same way.

Measured: delete flysight.txt, reboot: the recreated file contains BLE_IRK: 0000…0000. With the fix it contains random keys, unchanged after another reboot. The effect on an existing bond was not measured.

Proposed fix

Move the end of the function (device ID, key generation) into a helper called on both paths:

FlySight/state.c
+static void FS_State_Complete(void)
+{
+	/* Get device ID */
+	...
+	/* Initialize IRK if needed */
+	...
+	/* Initialize ERK if needed */
+	...
+}
+
 void FS_State_Read(void)
 {
 	...
 	if (f_open(&stateFile, "/flysight.txt", FA_READ) != FR_OK)
+	{
+		/* No state file yet: the keys must still be generated, or this boot would run
+		   with all-zero root keys and the next one with different ones */
+		FS_State_Complete();
 		return;
+	}
 	...
 	f_close(&stateFile);
-	/* Get device ID */ ... /* Initialize IRK if needed */ ... /* Initialize ERK if needed */ ...
+	FS_State_Complete();
 }

5.Tests

A script drives the board over SWD (button presses and, when the USB cable is connected, a simulated unplug). On the Mac, a small CoreBluetooth program scans, connects, and reads FT_Packet_In; on Android and on the iPad, the SkyGames app. The Mac and the phone were both bonded with the board before these runs; the iPad was paired during the second series.

iPad. The iPad held no bond with this board. The first pairing was done on an instrumented build with the Device Privacy call switched off: double-press, scan in the app, select the FlySight, accept the pairing request. The firmware recorded a connection from an RPA and a successful pairing. From then on the iPad reconnected in 1 to 4 seconds in every case tried: FlySight idle, FlySight back in pairing mode, after a FlySight reboot, with the Device Privacy call off and on, and on the four builds of table B. The app kept using the same identifier throughout.

The unused pairing window was also tried from the iPad, bonded, on the two develop builds: double-press, nothing connects, and the app's Connect button is pressed 246 s later. Unmodified: no connection within 25 s, then a new double-press and the iPad connected 2.5 s after it. With the patch: connected in 1.3 s.

Before that pairing, the app's scan listed this FlySight under a new identifier after each advertising restart (four identifiers over four runs). Two connection attempts made with an identifier obtained before the double-press never reached the FlySight: the firmware recorded no connection during the 30-second window.

Android. The phone was already bonded with the board for the runs of table A and table B; "connects" means the app shows the device as connected. A first pairing from the phone, followed by a new pairing window and a reconnection, was also done on an instrumented build with the Device Privacy call switched off, then on: it works in both cases.

Bug 1, same boot and same bond. On that instrumented build, switching only the Device Privacy call: on, the Mac connects (idle and pairing mode); off, it does not and the firmware records no event; on again, it connects.

Limits.

  • One board, one Mac, one Android phone, one iPad. No iPhone, and no older iOS version.
  • The Mac's failures with the FlySight idle were measured with a bond that already existed, including straight after a boot. A brand-new pairing on unmodified firmware followed at once by an idle attempt was not done.
  • No radio capture: that the controller drops the Mac's request is deduced from the absence of any event, the address the Mac is seen using, and the on/off/on comparison.
  • Bug 3 was tested on a working branch whose state.c is identical to master and develop, not on those builds themselves.
  • All test builds had CFG_DEBUGGER_SUPPORTED set to 1, which the SWD-driven bench needs.
  • In table A the USB cable stayed connected and unplugging was simulated by forcing the VBUS detection pin low over SWD; only master was run that way. Table B has all four builds with the cable really unplugged.
  • On the iPad, the connection address was recorded on the instrumented build only; on the four builds of table B the result is what the app shows.

Linux: the same tests from a Linux PC, and three more bugs

7.Linux: bugs 1 and 2 from a Linux PC, and what a Linux host needs to pair

Glossary

BlueZ
The Bluetooth stack of Linux. An application talks to it over D-Bus.
Pairing agent
An object that an application registers with BlueZ (org.bluez.AgentManager1.RegisterAgent), and that BlueZ asks to accept a pairing.
Library, SkyGames
"Library" is the native BLE layer of the SkyGames desktop application, driven by a script. "SkyGames" is the application itself.

Problem

Bugs 1 and 2. Linux is not affected by bug 1. The firmware sees the PC connect from its public identity address, with no RPA: the same kind of connection as the Mac's. It is accepted with and without the fix of bug 1, on the four builds of table C and on the instrumented build with the Device Privacy call switched off and on. That is the difference with the Mac: with its default configuration (Privacy off) a Linux host gives the FlySight no identity resolving key when it bonds, and the specification makes a controller ignore the identity address only of a peer that has given one. Bug 2 shows from Linux exactly as from the other hosts.

The first pairing. What stops a Linux host is on its own side. Without a pairing agent registered by the application, BlueZ refuses the pairing, whether it is triggered by reading FT_Packet_In or requested explicitly: the firmware records PAIRING_COMPLETE with status 2 and reason 0x0c, and closes the link at its 30-second timeout. With an agent, the pairing completes and the PC is bonded.

Proposed fix

Nothing to change in the firmware. One line in Docs/ble.md, where pairing is described (commit Linux: what a host needs to pair, test tools, results and log):

Docs/ble.md
+    *   **Linux (BlueZ):** Reading a secure characteristic is not enough there. BlueZ refuses the "Just Works"
+        pairing, whether triggered by that read or requested with `Device1.Pair`, unless the application has
+        registered a pairing agent (`org.bluez.AgentManager1.RegisterAgent`); without one the adapter is not
+        even bondable. With an agent registered, call `Device1.Pair` after connecting. Measured with BlueZ 5.72:
+        without an agent the FlySight reports a failed pairing (reason `0x0c`) and closes the link at the
+        30-second timeout.

In the file it is a single line; it is folded here.

8.Linux, bug 4: a reset while flysight.txt is being written changes the BLE keys

Found while testing from Linux, then reproduced on purpose. It is one commit on the branch, Keep the previous flysight.txt until the new one is written (ba7e1d8). It is not in the patch of section 6: the three fixes above do not need it.

Glossary

State file
/flysight.txt on the microSD card. Besides the settings a user may edit, it holds what the firmware must find again at the next start: BLE_IRK and BLE_ERK (see bug 3), Session_ID, Temp_Folder, Config_File. Session_ID is a random number drawn for each session, Temp_Folder the number of the folder of /temp the last session was written to, Config_File the configuration chosen in config mode.
Active mode, start mode, config mode
What the button starts from sleep. Active mode logs a jump; start mode takes its place when Active_Mode is 1 in the state file; config mode announces the files of the /config folder of the card one after the other, and a press chooses the one being announced. Each of those files names, on its Init_File line, the audio file that announces it.
FatFs, cluster, FAT
FatFs is the file-system library of the firmware. It stores a file in clusters and keeps the table of the free ones, the FAT, on the card. A card is full when no cluster is free.
FA_CREATE_ALWAYS
The FatFs option that opens a file for writing and, if it exists, empties it first.
FUS_Ver, Stack_Ver
Two lines of the state file: the versions of the radio firmware, which runs on the second processor of the microcontroller.
SWD
The debug link of the ST-Link probe. A script uses it to stop the firmware at a chosen instruction, read its memory, drive a pin or reset it.
VBUS
The 5 V of the USB cable. The firmware reads it on a pin to know whether the cable is in; holding that pin low over SWD makes it see the cable out.

Problem

FS_State_Init() runs at every start. It reads the state file, then writes it back from memory:

FlySight/state.c
void FS_State_Init(void)
{
	FS_ResourceManager_RequestResource(FS_RESOURCE_FATFS);
	FS_State_Read();
	FS_State_Write();                              // at every start
	FS_ResourceManager_ReleaseResource(FS_RESOURCE_FATFS);
}

static void FS_State_Write(void)
{
	...
	if (f_open(&stateFile, "/flysight.txt", FA_WRITE|FA_CREATE_ALWAYS) != FR_OK)   // the file is empty from here...
	...
	f_close(&stateFile);                                                          // ...to here
}

Between those two lines the card holds an empty flysight.txt, and a reset there leaves it empty. At the next start the firmware opens it, reads no BLE_IRK and no BLE_ERK, draws new ones and writes them. The change is permanent: bonded devices no longer recognise the FlySight and have to pair again, and the other values of the file are back to their defaults.

Measured on the bench build of the branch, with breakpoints on three starts: the write begins 125 ms after the reset, the file is emptied at 130 ms and closed at 145 ms. A reset sent over SWD with the firmware stopped in FS_State_Write() changed the keys 6 times out of 6. Each time the next start was caught at the call that draws the new IRK, and the root directory it had just read showed FLYSIGHT.TXT with a size of 0 and no cluster. Resets sent 50 ms earlier (4), after the file is closed (68) or with the card off (17) all kept the keys. The keys were compared by the SHA-1 of what the firmware holds in memory.

The same write is done in three other places, with the same window: when active mode or start mode starts (FS_State_NextSession(), which increments Temp_Folder and draws a new Session_ID), when config mode ends (FS_State_SetConfigFilename()) and after USB is unplugged (FS_State_Update()). A reset sent there with the firmware stopped just after the file is opened changed the keys every time, on master, on develop and on the branch without the fix: 6 times out of 6 as active mode starts, 6 out of 6 after a USB unplug, 5 out of 5 as a press leaves config mode, and once more with the cable pulled out by hand. The whole file is lost with the keys: Temp_Folder is back to 0000, and the sessions that follow are written over /temp/0001 and the next ones.

Proposed fix

Write the new state next to the previous one, and replace the previous one only once the new one is complete. A reset between the removal and the renaming leaves only the new file, which the next start puts in place. When the new file cannot be written, give it up at the first write that fails and write in place as before:

The hunks are those of the branch, on top of the patch of section 6: FS_State_Complete() is the helper of bug 3. On master and develop without that patch the change is the same, with return; alone at that place.

The last branch of FS_State_Write() is there for a card with no free cluster. The code before the fix gets through on such a card, because emptying the file frees the cluster it writes to next; with that branch a full card behaves as it did before the fix, window included.

The two checks in FS_State_WriteContent() both come from the way FatFs treats a write that finds no room: it does not report it as an error, and it searches the whole FAT before each one fails. Without the check of the last write, a good file would have been replaced by an incomplete one. With that check alone, which was a first version of the fix, writing the state took 43 s on a card that was really full, one search for each of 136 writes: the check of the first write was added for that.

The fix is in FS_State_Write(), so it covers the four places where the state is written. It protects the file from then on: it does not bring back a flysight.txt that a firmware without it has already emptied. What it costs, from the measurements below:

  • the write takes 10 to 20 ms longer: 31 to 43 ms where it took 20 to 24, and 27 to 36 ms where it took 19 to 20 after the cable was pulled out by hand. One measurement out of eighteen took 93 ms;
  • on a card with no free cluster it takes about a third of a second instead of 20 ms: one search of the FAT, then the write in place. That time grows with the size of the FAT;
  • a FLYSIGHT.TMP stays on the card after a write that a reset interrupted, until the next write of the state.

9.Linux, bug 5: a command dropped behind the last file acknowledgement

One commit on the branch, Do not drop a command that follows the last file acknowledgement (fa2a4ad). It is not in the patch of section 6.

Glossary

File transfer
The service a host uses to read files from the card. The host writes commands to FT_Packet_In (READ, and one FILE_ACK per packet it receives); the FlySight answers on FT_Packet_Out (ACK or NAK, then the FILE_DATA packets).
Receive queue
Where the packets written by the host wait until the task FS_CRS_Update() takes them one by one with Custom_CRS_GetNextRxPacket().
Idle state, reading state
The two states of that task that matter here. FS_CRS_State_Idle() handles commands. FS_CRS_State_Read() runs while a file is being sent and handles only FILE_ACK and CANCEL.

Problem

FS_CRS_State_Read() empties the receive queue in one pass, and only afterwards decides that the file is done:

FlySight/crs.c
while ((next_state == FS_CRS_STATE_READ) && (packet = Custom_CRS_GetNextRxPacket()))
{
	switch (packet->data[0])
	{
	case FS_CRS_COMMAND_CANCEL:   ...
	case FS_CRS_COMMAND_FILE_ACK: ... ++next_ack; ...
	}                                              // any other packet: taken out of the queue, and nothing
}
...
if (next_ack == last_packet)                       // decided once the queue is empty
{
	next_state = FS_CRS_STATE_IDLE;
}

A command that is already in the queue behind the acknowledgement of the last packet is therefore taken out while the state is still "reading", matches no case, and is dropped without an answer. A host that asks for a second file as soon as the first one is complete then waits for nothing.

Measured from Linux, where BlueZ sends the acknowledgement and the next request back to back. SkyGames reads FLYSIGHT.TXT then CONFIG.TXT at each connection: the request for the second file got no answer in 6 connections out of 12 on the branch, and in 3 out of 16 on unmodified master and develop. Before the application learned to ask again, such a connection ended with CONFIG.TXT not read after 20 s (2 connections out of 10). It was not looked for from macOS, iOS or Android.

Proposed fix

Stop reading the queue once the whole file is acknowledged, and call the update task again so that the idle state handles what is waiting:

FlySight/crs.c
 			case FS_CRS_COMMAND_FILE_ACK:
 				...
+
+				if (next_ack == last_packet)
+				{
+					// The whole file is acknowledged. A packet waiting behind
+					// this one is a new command: leave it for the idle state
+					next_state = FS_CRS_STATE_IDLE;
+				}
 				break;
 ...
 	if (next_state == FS_CRS_STATE_IDLE)
 	{
 		...
 		// De-initialize disk
 		FS_ResourceManager_ReleaseResource(FS_RESOURCE_FATFS);
+
+		// Call update task, in case a command is already waiting
+		UTIL_SEQ_SetTask(1<<CFG_TASK_FS_CRS_UPDATE_ID, CFG_SCH_PRIO_1);
 	}

Measured with the fix: no request lost in 40 connections. Independently of the firmware, the application now sends a read request again when nothing answers it within 2 s, which is what makes it work with a firmware that does not have this fix (20 connections out of 20, 8 of them after a second request).

10.Linux, bug 6: a microSD card powered again too soon locks the FlySight

One commit on the branch, Keep VCC off for 50 ms once it is disabled (d94ea11). It is not in the patch of section 6.

Glossary

VCC_EN
The pin that switches the supply of the microSD card. VCC_Init() sets it, VCC_DeInit() clears it.
Resource manager
resource_manager.c. It counts the users of the supply, of the card and of FatFs. The card is mounted when the first user asks for it (FatFS_Init()), and unmounted and switched off when the last one lets go: at the end of each file transfer, in particular.
Error_Handler()
Disables the interrupts, turns the LED red and loops for ever. Only a reset gets the firmware out of it.

Problem

A file command that follows another one finds the card just switched off, and switches it on again at once:

FlySight/resource_manager.c
static void VCC_DeInit(void)
{
	...
	HAL_GPIO_WritePin(VCC_EN_GPIO_Port, VCC_EN_Pin, GPIO_PIN_RESET);   // end of a transfer: card off
	...
}

static FS_ResourceManager_Result_t FatFS_Init(void)                    // next command: card on, then
{
	...
	if (f_mount(&fs, "0:/", 1) != FR_OK)
	{
		Error_Handler();                                               // never returns
	}
	...
}

When the card stays without power for about 1 ms it does not restart cleanly, f_mount() fails, and the FlySight stops advertising and answers nothing until it is reset.

Measured with an experiment in the firmware alone: mount the card, cut its power for a chosen time, mount it again, six times per duration.

Time without powerSecond mount
0 to 0.7 ms (8 durations)6 of 6 OK each
1.0 ms2 of 6 fail
1.3 ms6 of 6 fail
1.6 to 45 ms (10 durations)6 of 6 OK each

Each failure is the same, and is what a firmware locked up during a BLE session held in memory when it was read over SWD: the card had answered its initialisation but had been taken for a standard-capacity card when it is a high-capacity one, had refused the switch to high speed, and the sector read as sector 0 was not sector 0, so FatFs found no file system.

Over BLE, a firmware that records each of its mounts shows how close a session comes. In 16 normal connections on master and develop, the request for the second file found the card less than 1 ms after its power was cut in 13 of them, and 2 s after in the 3 where the request had to be sent again. With a client that received every notification twice and answered in bursts, it was 0.5 to 2 ms, and the firmware locked up in 1 such session out of 4 on master and 2 out of 4 on master + patch. The lockup was never seen in a normal session.

Proposed fix

Keep the supply off long enough for the rail to discharge before anything can switch it on again:

FlySight/resource_manager.c
+// Time VCC stays off once disabled, so that the rail discharges
+#define VCC_OFF_MSEC 50
 ...
 static void VCC_DeInit(void)
 {
 	...
 			/* Disable VCC */
 			HAL_GPIO_WritePin(VCC_EN_GPIO_Port, VCC_EN_Pin, GPIO_PIN_RESET);
 			...
+
+			/* Keep VCC off until the rail has discharged. A microSD card
+			 * powered again about 1 ms after losing power does not restart
+			 * cleanly, and the mount that follows fails */
+			HAL_Delay(VCC_OFF_MSEC);
 	...
 }

Measured with the fix: the same experiment gives 60 mounts out of 60, the 1.0 and 1.3 ms ones included. master + patch, which had just locked up at the second of the sessions with doubled notifications, went through 12 of them with the fix, and the branch through 8.

The 50 ms are a margin over what was measured on one board with one card, 30 times the longest gap that failed; the rail was not looked at with an oscilloscope. Every time the supply is switched off now takes 50 ms longer, the end of active mode included. A card that cannot be mounted still ends in Error_Handler(), as everywhere in the firmware. A reset with the card powered, which the fix does not change, did no harm in 68 tries. Details and scripts are in linux/README.md.
